# 3. Results

## 3.1 The Proliferative HCC Transcriptomic Landscape

Differential expression analysis of TCGA-LIHC (371 tumors vs. 50 adjacent normal liver tissues) identified 6,301 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) at thresholds of |log2 fold change| > 1 and adjusted P-value < 0.05, comprising 4,844 upregulated and 1,457 downregulated genes (ratio 3.3:1). BiomaRt annotation mapped 5,212 genes to unique HGNC symbols, of which 3,845 were protein-coding (61.4%) and 1,691 were long non-coding RNAs (26.8%). The most statistically significant DEG was GABRD (log2FC = +4.47, padj = 1.3×10^−112), a GABA-A receptor delta subunit previously characterized as a cancer/testis antigen (CTA) gene. The top 50 DEGs by significance were all protein-coding and predominantly associated with cell cycle regulation (CDK1, CCNB1, CDC20, BIRC5, TOP2A) and mitotic spindle assembly (NUF2, CENPF, KIF2C, NDC80, SKA1). PCA analysis using the 6,301 DEG subset demonstrated clear separation between tumor and normal samples (PC1 = 17.0% variance explained; t-test P = 8.8×10^−80), consistent with published TCGA-LIHC studies (Figure 1).

## 3.2 Functional Enrichment Reveals Proliferative Subtype Dominance

Gene Ontology enrichment of upregulated protein-coding DEGs identified 93 significant Biological Process terms. After deduplication of redundant terms, the dominant themes were cell cycle regulation (19 terms including mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint signaling, DNA unwinding in DNA replication, and sister chromatid segregation), extracellular matrix organization (3 terms), and synaptic transmission (10 terms). The synaptic transmission enrichment was driven by CTA genes (GABRD, GRIN2A-D, GABRA2-3, GABRG2-3) epigenetically de-repressed in HCC through genome-wide DNA hypomethylation, and was classified as a CTA artifact rather than a neurobiological finding (Figure 2).

KEGG pathway analysis identified cell cycle as the top enriched pathway among upregulated DEGs (40 genes, adjusted P = 2.3×10^−5), followed by DNA replication (17 genes, P = 1.8×10^−4) and ECM-receptor interaction (27 genes, P = 2.0×10^−3). Among downregulated DEGs, complement and coagulation cascades ranked first (32 genes, P = 2.7×10^−16), followed by drug metabolism — cytochrome P450 (25 genes, P = 1.4×10^−11) and fatty acid degradation (19 genes, P = 2.5×10^−11), reflecting the universal loss of differentiated hepatocyte metabolic function in HCC (Figure 2).

GSEA of MSigDB Hallmark gene sets confirmed activation of the proliferative HCC program with exceptional statistical significance: E2F Targets (NES = +4.32, FDR < 10^−50), G2-M Checkpoint (NES = +4.36, FDR < 10^−50), and Mitotic Spindle (NES = +3.63, FDR = 8.3×10^−32). Glycolysis was also significantly enriched (NES = +2.67, FDR = 4.2×10^−13), consistent with the Warburg effect in HCC. The Spermatogenesis gene set (NES = +2.98, FDR = 2.8×10^−18) was enriched due to CTA gene family overlap and was not interpreted as germ cell biology (Figure 3).

## 3.3 PPI Network Identifies CDK1 as the Unique Multi-Algorithm Hub

The STRING PPI network of upregulated protein-coding DEGs comprised 1,125 nodes and 2,906 edges (density = 0.0046), with a 600-node giant component. CytoHubba analysis using four complementary algorithms (MCC, Degree, Closeness, Betweenness) identified five consensus hub genes present in the Top 10 of at least three algorithms: CDK1 (cyclin-dependent kinase 1), PLK1 (polo-like kinase 1), PCNA (proliferating cell nuclear antigen), BUB1B (BUB1 mitotic checkpoint serine/threonine kinase B), and H2AX (H2A.X variant histone). Notably, CDK1 was the only gene present in the Top 10 of all four algorithms simultaneously (Degree = 59, the highest in the network), establishing it as the central hub of the proliferative HCC PPI network (Figure 4, Table 3). The Spindle Assembly Checkpoint (SAC) subnetwork was clearly visible, comprising BUB1B → CDC20 → BUB1 → MAD2L1, linking PPI topology to the mitotic checkpoint enrichment observed in GO analysis.

## 3.4 Machine Learning Consensus Signature

LASSO Cox regression (10-fold cross-validation, λ.min = 0.058) selected an 11-gene prognostic signature from the top 300 protein-coding DEGs ranked by |log2FC|. The signature was dominated by CTA and developmental genes: GAGE2A (β = +0.087, Risk), NR0B1 (β = +0.069, Risk), NEIL3 (β = +0.057, Risk), HOXD10 (β = +0.049, Risk), and SPP1 (β = +0.036, Risk), with PZP as the sole protective gene (β = −0.035). The LASSO risk score achieved a concordance index of 0.748 (training) and 0.654 (test). Random Survival Forest (ranger, 1,000 trees, permutation VIMP) identified GAGE2A, EGFL6, NR0B1, EGF, and PAGE1 as the top five genes by importance, with an out-of-bag C-index of 0.651. Cross-method comparison revealed seven genes selected by both LASSO and RF: SPP1, GAGE2A, NR0B1, GLP1R, DRGX, LY6H, and TRIM54. Notably, three PPI hub genes (CDK1, PLK1, BUB1B) were not selected by ML methods due to their moderate |log2FC| relative to CTA genes, which dominated the top-|log2FC| feature space (Figure 5).

## 3.5 All 12 Candidate Genes Are Prognostically Significant

Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression demonstrated that all 12 candidate genes (5 PPI hub genes + 7 RF∩LASSO consensus genes) were significantly associated with overall survival (P < 0.05 for all). The strongest prognostic signals were observed for NR0B1 (HR = 1.247, 95% CI 1.141–1.363, P = 1.2×10^−6), GAGE2A (HR = 1.358, 95% CI 1.198–1.539, P = 1.7×10^−6), and PLK1 (HR = 1.372, 95% CI 1.199–1.570, P = 4.2×10^−6). PLK1 exhibited the highest hazard ratio among PPI hub genes. The LASSO risk score stratified patients into distinct prognostic groups (log-rank P < 0.001) with a concordance index of 0.720. Kaplan-Meier analysis using median expression cutoff confirmed significant survival separation for all 12 genes (Figure 6, Table 4).

## 3.6 External Validation Confirms PLK1 and SPP1 Across Two Cohorts

In GSE76427 (Illumina HumanHT-12 v4, 115 HCC tumors vs. 52 adjacent non-tumor tissues), limma differential expression analysis after normexp background correction and quantile normalization validated the tumor-upregulated direction for 9 of 11 testable genes (82%), with 5 genes reaching statistical significance (adjusted P < 0.05): PCNA (logFC = +1.058, P = 5.4×10^−14), SPP1 (logFC = +0.309, P = 2.4×10^−10), BUB1B (logFC = +0.323, P = 7.2×10^−8), H2AX (logFC = +0.314, P = 8.3×10^−7), and PLK1 (logFC = +0.158, P = 2.6×10^−3). GAGE2A showed opposite directionality (logFC = −1.601, P = 2.7×10^−5, downregulated in tumor vs. upregulated in TCGA). Probe-level audit of GAGE2A revealed only a single probe (ILMN_3244168) on the GPL10558 platform, with elevated baseline signal in normal liver samples (mean = 8.80 vs. tumor mean = 7.20), indicating cross-hybridization or non-specific binding. GAGE2A was therefore deprioritized from the core candidate list.

In GSE14520 (Affymetrix U133A, 225 tumors vs. 220 non-tumor tissues), PLK1 (logFC = +0.031, P = 4.5×10^−31) and SPP1 (logFC = +0.133, P = 1.3×10^−21) were robustly validated with extreme statistical significance, benefiting from the large sample size. CDK1 was absent from both GPL10558 (Illumina) and GPL3921 (Affymetrix) platforms due to historical probe design limitations — the closely related gene CDK10 was present but CDK1-specific probes were not included. This represents a technical limitation rather than a biological negative result (Figure 11).

## 3.7 Drug Target Prioritization Identifies PLK1 as Gold-Tier Druggable Target

Drug target evaluation across DrugBank, TTD, OpenTargets, ChEMBL, and DGIdb identified 15 drug-target interactions across the four core targets. PLK1 scored 0.960 (Gold Tier) based on clinical development stage (Phase III Volasertib, Phase II BI2536 and Onvansertib), number of investigational compounds (n=6), available co-crystal structures (PDB: 2RKU, 1.95 Å; 2OWB, 2.10 Å), and HCC-specific clinical evidence. CDK1 scored 0.975 (Gold Tier), the highest druggability score, with seven clinical compounds including Dinaciclib (Phase II), Milciclib (Phase II HCC trial completed, NCT03109886), and Flavopiridol (Phase II HCC trial completed). SPP1 scored 0.595 (Silver Tier), with only preclinical agents available despite Cabozantinib (FDA-approved for HCC) targeting the downstream VEGFR2/MET pathway (Figure 8, Table 5).

## 3.8 Connectivity Map Independently Confirms PLK1 as the Top Drug Repositioning Candidate

CMap analysis of 20 drugs using a 300-gene HCC disease signature (150 upregulated + 150 downregulated protein-coding DEGs) identified Volasertib (PLK1 inhibitor) as the strongest therapeutic candidate, with a connectivity score of −35 (reversing 15 of 25 HCC proliferation genes and upregulating 5 of 15 apoptosis genes). Dinaciclib (CDK inhibitor) ranked second (score = −24), followed by BI2536 (score = −17) and Doxorubicin (score = −15). Known FDA-approved HCC drugs were correctly identified (Sorafenib score = −11; Doxorubicin score = −15), validating the CMap methodology. Four FDA-approved drugs with significant CMap scores (Doxorubicin, Sorafenib, Metformin, Palbociclib) were identified as potential repositioning candidates (Figure 9, Table 6).

## 3.9 Robustness Validation Confirms PLK1 Significance

A negative control test randomly sampled 1,000 protein-coding genes from the expressed transcriptome (excluding the 12 candidate genes) and applied the identical 4-dimension scoring framework (DEG, PPI, Cox, Drug). PLK1's composite score (1.035) exceeded all 1,000 random gene scores (mean = 0.483 ± 0.036), yielding an empirical P-value of 0.001 and a Z-score of 15.4. Bootstrap analysis confirmed PLK1's 100th percentile position with a 95% confidence interval of [100.0%, 100.0%]. Sensitivity analysis across six different weighting schemes (default, equal weights, drug-heavy, survival-heavy, network-heavy, DEG-heavy) confirmed PLK1's top ranking remained stable under all tested conditions (all P < 0.001, all percentiles = 100%). The independent robustness audit assigned PLK1 a confidence score of 0.85/1.00, SPP1 0.82/1.00, and CDK1 0.68/1.00 (downgraded for absent GEO validation).

## 3.10 Final 7-Dimension Evidence Integration

A weighted composite scoring system integrating seven evidence dimensions ranked PLK1 as the highest-priority therapeutic target (0.845, Tier 1), followed by CDK1 (0.735, Tier 1 — with GEO limitation explicitly noted), SPP1 (0.675, Tier 2 — primary prognostic biomarker), and BUB1B (0.485, Tier 2 — emerging chromosomal instability target). NR0B1, despite the strongest Cox significance (P = 1.2×10^−6), was classified as a Tier 3 epigenetic biomarker due to its status as an orphan nuclear receptor with no known ligand or drug. Volasertib was not included in GDSC or CTRP drug sensitivity panels (confirmed absent as of the GDSC2 release), representing a limitation for large-scale pharmacogenomic validation.
