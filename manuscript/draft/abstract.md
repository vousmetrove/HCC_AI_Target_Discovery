# Abstract

**Background:** Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains the third leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide, yet systemic therapeutic options are limited by drug resistance and low response rates. Systematic multi-dimensional evidence integration for therapeutic target discovery remains an unmet need in HCC research.

**Methods:** We established a 9-level AI-assisted evidence framework integrating TCGA-LIHC transcriptomic profiling (371 tumors vs. 50 normal tissues), protein-protein interaction network analysis (STRING v12, 1,125 nodes, 2,906 edges), machine learning feature selection (LASSO Cox + Random Forest), multi-cohort external validation (GSE76427, Illumina; GSE14520, Affymetrix), and Connectivity Map (CMap) drug repurposing analysis. A negative control test (1,000 random genes) and independent robustness audit were performed to assess result reliability.

**Results:** Differential expression analysis identified 6,301 DEGs (4,844 upregulated, 1,457 downregulated) with dominant proliferative HCC subtype signatures (E2F Targets NES=+4.32; G2M Checkpoint NES=+4.36, both FDR < 10^−50). PLK1 emerged as the highest-confidence therapeutic target, positive across all 9 evidence levels: TCGA Cox HR=1.372 (P=4.2×10^−6), external validation in GSE76427 (P=2.6×10^−3) and GSE14520 (P=4.5×10^−31), Gold druggability (0.960), and strongest CMap proliferation reversal (Volasertib score=−35). A negative control test confirmed PLK1's significance was not attributable to algorithmic bias (empirical P=0.001, Z=15.4, stable across 6 weight schemes). SPP1 was validated as a robust prognostic biomarker (GEO P=1.3×10^−11, both ML methods selected). CDK1 was identified as the central PPI hub (degree=59, present in all four CytoHubba algorithms) but lacked external validation due to historical probe absence on both microarray platforms. Two candidate genes were intentionally deprioritized: GAGE2A (GEO direction opposite, single probe) and NR0B1 (strongest Cox P=1.2×10^−6 but orphan nuclear receptor with no known drug).

**Conclusions:** This multi-dimensional AI-assisted framework prioritizes PLK1 as a high-confidence therapeutic target and SPP1 as a companion prognostic biomarker for proliferative HCC. The CMap analysis independently confirms PLK1 inhibition with Volasertib (Phase III) as a strong drug repositioning strategy. The framework's systematic evidence integration, honest reporting of negative findings, and public code availability provide a generalizable template for AI-assisted target discovery in other cancers.

**Keywords:** hepatocellular carcinoma; therapeutic target discovery; PLK1; SPP1; connectivity map; multi-omics integration; drug repurposing; evidence-based bioinformatics
