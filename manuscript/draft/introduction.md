# 1. Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common primary liver malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately 830,000 deaths annually [1]. The majority of patients present with advanced-stage disease unsuitable for curative resection or transplantation, leaving systemic therapy as the primary treatment modality. Current first-line systemic therapies include the multi-kinase inhibitors sorafenib and lenvatinib, and the combination of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab [2]. Second-line options include regorafenib, cabozantinib, and ramucirumab. Despite these advances, objective response rates remain below 30% for most regimens, drug resistance is nearly universal, and median overall survival for advanced HCC remains under two years [3]. This therapeutic landscape underscores the urgent need for novel therapeutic targets and biomarker-driven treatment strategies.

The advent of large-scale cancer genomics projects, particularly The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), has provided unprecedented opportunities for computational target discovery. The TCGA-LIHC project characterized the genomic landscape of 363 HCC cases, identifying three molecular subtypes (iCluster 1–3) with distinct prognostic and biological features [4]. The proliferative subtype (iCluster 1) is characterized by cell cycle dysregulation, TP53 mutations, and poor prognosis, while the non-proliferative subtypes (iCluster 2–3) are associated with CTNNB1 mutations, metabolic pathway alterations, and more favorable outcomes. Hoshida et al. independently classified HCC into three transcriptional subclasses (S1–S3), with the S2 proliferative subclass showing activation of Myc/Akt pathways and the poorest survival [5]. These landmark studies established that HCC is a molecularly heterogeneous disease, yet the translational gap between molecular subclassification and actionable therapeutic targets remains largely unaddressed.

Traditional approaches to therapeutic target discovery in HCC have typically followed a linear paradigm: identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs), select a few candidates based on fold-change or significance ranking, and validate in vitro. This approach suffers from several limitations. First, DEG ranking alone does not prioritize genes with network-level importance — a gene with moderate fold-change may be a central network hub, while a gene with extreme fold-change may be a passenger event. Second, single-cohort findings often fail external validation, particularly when platform-specific artifacts are present. Third, most DEG-based studies stop at biomarker identification without assessing druggability or drug repositioning potential. Fourth, negative results and contradictory findings are rarely reported, creating a publication bias toward positive findings.

To address these limitations, we developed a multi-layer AI-assisted evidence framework that integrates nine independent evidence dimensions organized into a three-stage pipeline: Discovery (DEG analysis, functional enrichment, PPI network construction), Validation (machine learning feature selection, survival analysis, multi-cohort external validation), and Prioritization (drug target evaluation, Connectivity Map analysis, robustness testing). This framework was applied to TCGA-LIHC transcriptomic data with external validation in two independent GEO cohorts (GSE76427, GSE14520). A key feature of our approach is the systematic documentation of contradictory evidence and the use of negative control testing to distinguish true biological signals from algorithmic artifacts.

In this study, we demonstrate that PLK1 (polo-like kinase 1) emerges as the highest-confidence therapeutic target, supported by all nine evidence levels including independent pharmacological validation through Connectivity Map analysis. SPP1 (osteopontin) is validated as a robust prognostic biomarker with companion diagnostic potential. CDK1 is identified as the central PPI network hub, though its external validation is limited by historical probe design on microarray platforms. Importantly, we report the intentional deprioritization of two candidate genes (GAGE2A and NR0B1) based on contradictory evidence, demonstrating the framework's capacity to avoid false-positive target nominations. This study provides both a validated set of HCC therapeutic targets and a generalizable computational framework for evidence-based target discovery.
