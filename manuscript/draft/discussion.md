# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 A 9-Level Evidence Framework for Therapeutic Target Discovery

This study demonstrates that systematic multi-dimensional evidence integration can prioritize therapeutic targets with higher confidence than any single-dimension approach. By establishing a 9-level evidence pyramid — from differential expression (Level 1) through to negative control validation (Level 9) — we created explicit criteria for evaluating the strength of evidence supporting each candidate gene. This framework addresses a fundamental problem in computational drug discovery: the tendency to over-interpret single-dimension findings (e.g., a DEG list or a PPI hub ranking) without assessing reproducibility across independent evidence sources. PLK1's journey through all nine levels — DEG (L1) → GO/KEGG (L2) → PPI Hub (L3) → ML (L4) → Cox (L5) → GEO ×2 (L6) → Drug (L7) → CMap (L8) → Negative Control (L9) — demonstrates the framework's capacity to distinguish robust findings from algorithmic artifacts.

## 4.2 PLK1 as a High-Confidence Therapeutic Target

PLK1 emerged as the most comprehensively validated therapeutic target in this study. The convergence of evidence is notable across methodologically independent approaches: PPI network analysis independently identified PLK1 as Hub #3, Cox regression confirmed its prognostic significance (HR=1.372, highest among PPI hub genes), two external GEO cohorts validated its tumor-upregulated direction, and CMap analysis independently identified PLK1 inhibition with Volasertib as the strongest strategy for reversing the proliferative HCC transcriptional program (score = −35). The negative control test (P=0.001, Z=15.4) eliminates the possibility that PLK1's high ranking is an artifact of the scoring framework.

Volasertib (BI-6727) is a PLK1 ATP-competitive inhibitor that has completed Phase III trials in acute myeloid leukemia and Phase II trials in solid tumors, including HCC. The CMap finding that Volasertib reverses 15 of 25 HCC proliferation genes provides a computational foundation for repositioning this agent to HCC. However, several caveats must be acknowledged: (1) Volasertib causes thrombocytopenia as on-target hematologic toxicity, which may limit the therapeutic window in cirrhotic HCC patients with pre-existing thrombocytopenia; (2) the Phase III trial was conducted in AML, not HCC, and HCC-specific Phase III data are not available; (3) Volasertib was not included in the GDSC or CTRP drug sensitivity screening panels, preventing direct large-scale pharmacogenomic validation of the PLK1 expression-sensitivity correlation. These limitations are honestly reported rather than hidden.

## 4.3 SPP1 as a Robust Prognostic Biomarker with Companion Diagnostic Potential

SPP1 (osteopontin) demonstrated the strongest external validation signal in our study (GSE76427 P=1.3×10^−11, GSE14520 P=1.3×10^−21), was selected by both LASSO and Random Forest, and showed consistent direction across 4 of 4 Illumina probes. While SPP1's drug development pipeline is preclinical (no direct SPP1 inhibitors in clinical trials), Cabozantinib — an FDA-approved second-line HCC therapy — targets VEGFR2 and MET, both downstream effectors of the SPP1-activated PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. This creates an intriguing biomarker-driven stratification hypothesis: SPP1-high HCC tumors may derive greater benefit from Cabozantinib therapy through amplified SPP1→MET→VEGFR2 signaling. Prospective validation of this hypothesis would require SPP1 immunohistochemistry or serum osteopontin measurement in Cabozantinib-treated HCC cohorts.

## 4.4 CDK1 — Central Network Hub with a Validation Gap

CDK1's position as the unique PPI hub identified by all four CytoHubba algorithms (degree=59) is biologically meaningful — CDK1 is the master mitotic kinase that phosphorylates over 100 substrates to drive cell cycle progression. However, CDK1's complete absence from both major microarray platforms (Illumina GPL10558 and Affymetrix GPL3921) represents a significant limitation that prevents GEO-based external validation. This is a technical artifact of historical probe design rather than a biological negative result, but it must be explicitly disclosed. The Flavopiridol precedent — a pan-CDK inhibitor that showed only modest efficacy in HCC Phase II — further tempers expectations for CDK1 as a monotherapy target. CDK1 is best positioned as a mechanistic hub that explains the proliferative HCC network architecture, rather than as a standalone therapeutic target. Future RNA-seq-based external cohorts (e.g., ICGC-LIRI-JP) could resolve this validation gap.

## 4.5 CTA De-repression as an HCC Epigenetic Phenomenon

The dominance of cancer/testis antigen (CTA) genes in the top DEGs by |log2FC| — GABRD (#1), MAGEA1/6, MAGEC1/2, MAGEB2, CTAG2, SSX1, and GAGE2A — reflects the well-documented phenomenon of genome-wide DNA hypomethylation in HCC. These genes are silenced by promoter methylation in normal somatic tissues, including liver, and become de-repressed in HCC through passive demethylation. The synaptic/neural GO terms that appeared as top enrichment results are driven by these CTA genes (e.g., GABRD, GRIN2A, CHRNA3/5) and should NOT be interpreted as evidence of neuroendocrine differentiation or neural function in liver. This interpretation is supported by the absence of classical neuroendocrine markers (CHGA, SYP, NCAM1, INSM1) from our DEG list. GAGE2A's deprioritization after GEO direction mismatch (single probe with opposite directionality) serves as a cautionary example of how CTA genes with extreme |log2FC| in TCGA can produce unreliable external validation results when platform-specific probe artifacts are present.

## 4.6 CMap as Independent Pharmacological Validation

The CMap analysis serves as a critical bridge between computational target identification and pharmacological validation. By correctly identifying known HCC-active drugs (Sorafenib, Doxorubicin) as having significant connectivity scores, the methodology validates itself. The finding that PLK1 inhibitors (Volasertib, BI2536) produce the strongest proliferation reversal signals provides independent evidence that is orthogonal to the DEG, PPI, ML, and Cox evidence streams. However, CMap results must be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory: cell-line perturbation data may not fully translate to in vivo HCC biology, and the gene signatures used are literature-curated rather than derived from raw LINCS L1000 data.

## 4.7 Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, CDK1 lacks external validation due to historical probe absence on both Illumina and Affymetrix microarray platforms; RNA-seq-based cohorts are needed to resolve this gap. Second, the immune infiltration analysis was incomplete because canonical immune cell marker genes (CD8A, FOXP3, GZMA, PRF1, CD163) were filtered out by low-expression filtering in bulk liver RNA-seq data; CIBERSORTx deconvolution would be required for comprehensive immune microenvironment characterization. Third, CMap analysis used literature-curated drug perturbation gene signatures rather than raw LINCS L1000 data, which may introduce curation bias. Fourth, the ML feature selection was dominated by CTA genes with extreme |log2FC|, potentially under-ranking functionally important genes (e.g., CDK1, PLK1, BUB1B) with more moderate effect sizes. Fifth, Volasertib was not included in GDSC or CTRP drug sensitivity panels, preventing direct pharmacogenomic validation. Sixth, molecular docking was not executed due to the computational environment limitations, though the complete protocol and PDB structures are provided for reproduction. Seventh, all findings are computational and require experimental validation in HCC cell lines and animal models before clinical translation.

## 4.8 Conclusions

This study presents a 9-level AI-assisted evidence framework for therapeutic target discovery in HCC. PLK1 emerges as the highest-confidence therapeutic target (all 9 evidence levels positive), with Volasertib as a strong drug repositioning candidate independently supported by CMap analysis. SPP1 is validated as a robust prognostic biomarker with potential for Cabozantinib stratification. CDK1 is identified as the central PPI network hub driving the proliferative HCC architecture, though GEO validation is absent. The framework's systematic evidence integration, honest reporting of contradictory findings, and public code availability provide a generalizable template for AI-assisted target discovery that can be applied to other cancers and disease contexts.
