# AI-Assisted Multi-Layer Evidence Framework for Therapeutic Target Discovery and Drug Repurposing in Hepatocellular Carcinoma

## Research Statement (for PhD Application / CV)

**Author:** Vousmetrove | **Date:** 2026-06-24

---

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains the third leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide, yet systemic therapeutic options remain limited. While large-scale transcriptomic datasets such as TCGA have greatly expanded our understanding of HCC molecular subtypes, the translational gap between differential gene expression lists and actionable therapeutic targets remains a fundamental challenge. This project addresses that gap by establishing a **9-level AI-assisted evidence framework** that systematically integrates transcriptomic profiling, protein-protein interaction networks, machine learning, multi-cohort external validation, and drug repurposing analysis for therapeutic target discovery.

**Methodology.** The framework operates through three sequential stages: Discovery, Validation, and Prioritization. In the Discovery stage, differential expression analysis (DESeq2) of TCGA-LIHC (371 tumors vs. 50 normal tissues) identified 6,301 DEGs. Functional enrichment (GO/KEGG/GSEA) revealed dominant proliferative subtype signatures (E2F Targets NES=+4.32; G2M Checkpoint NES=+4.36). Protein-protein interaction network construction (STRING v12, 1,125 nodes, 2,906 edges) combined with four CytoHubba algorithms (MCC, Degree, Closeness, Betweenness) identified five consensus hub genes. Machine learning feature selection was performed using LASSO Cox regression (glmnet, 10-fold CV) and Random Survival Forest (ranger, 1,000 trees, permutation VIMP). In the Validation stage, all 12 candidate hub genes were confirmed as significant in univariate Cox regression (P<0.05). External validation was conducted in two independent GEO cohorts: GSE76427 (Illumina HumanHT-12 v4, 115 tumors) and GSE14520 (Affymetrix U133A, 225 tumors). Drug repurposing analysis integrated DrugBank, TTD, OpenTargets, ChEMBL, and DGIdb databases, complemented by Connectivity Map (CMap) analysis with mechanism-based connectivity scoring. In the Prioritization stage, a weighted 7-dimension evidence scoring system integrated expression, network centrality, ML selection, survival impact, external validation, druggability, and immune microenvironment evidence.

**Key Findings.** PLK1 emerged as the top-ranked therapeutic target with the most complete evidence package: positive across all nine evidence levels, validated in both external GEO cohorts (GSE76427 P=2.6×10⁻³; GSE14520 P=4.5×10⁻³¹), and independently confirmed by CMap analysis (Volasertib score = −35, strongest proliferation reversal among 20 drugs tested). A negative control test (1,000 random genes, identical scoring framework) confirmed that PLK1's high score is not attributable to algorithmic bias (empirical P=0.001, Z=15.4). SPP1 was validated as a robust prognostic biomarker (GEO P=1.3×10⁻¹¹, both ML methods selected, 4/4 probes consistent in external validation). CDK1 was identified as the central PPI network hub (degree=59, present in all four CytoHubba Top 10 lists) but lacks GEO validation due to historical probe absence on both Illumina and Affymetrix platforms. Importantly, GAGE2A was intentionally deprioritized after a probe-level audit revealed opposite directionality in the external cohort, and NR0B1 was reclassified from "target" to "biomarker" due to its status as an orphan nuclear receptor with no known ligand or drug.

**Methodological Contributions.** (1) A transparent 9-level evidence pyramid providing explicit criteria for classifying findings from "DEG" to "Validated Therapeutic Target." (2) Cross-platform external validation strategy (Illumina + Affymetrix) with probe-level auditing to detect platform-specific artifacts. (3) Mechanism-based CMap scoring assessing drug perturbation effects on HCC proliferation gene programs. (4) Systematic negative control testing demonstrating that top-ranked findings are not scoring artifacts. (5) Honest reporting of data gaps, including the absence of Volasertib from GDSC/CTRP drug sensitivity panels and the incomplete status of immune infiltration analysis—findings that are documented rather than hidden.

**Significance.** This project demonstrates that systematic multi-dimensional evidence integration can prioritize therapeutic targets with higher confidence than any single-dimension approach. The PLK1-Volasertib and SPP1-Cabozantinib axes identified here provide actionable hypotheses for biomarker-driven clinical trials in HCC. The framework itself is generalizable to other cancers and disease contexts. All code, intermediate results, and documentation are publicly available on GitHub (github.com/vousmetrove/HCC_AI_Target_Discovery), ensuring full reproducibility.

**Keywords:** AI-assisted drug discovery, hepatocellular carcinoma, therapeutic target prioritization, PLK1, connectivity map, multi-omics integration, evidence-based bioinformatics
